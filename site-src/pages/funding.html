Funding

48 calls that can fund ME/CFS research, the ones worth applying to now, and which proposed experiment each one fits.

Checked 2026-10-29. Deadlines change; confirm on the funder's page before applying.

Apply now

  1. Anthropic guest investigators about 5 Nov 2026. Eight to twelve weeks working with Anthropic researchers on public data; principal investigators only. Best for re-analysing existing data or building an open crash-tracking tool.
  2. Solve M.E. Ramsay grants 13 Nov 2026. $50k–150k over one to two years, for early-career researchers, researchers new to ME/CFS and collaborations, with no country limit. Best for a pilot of crash-window sampling, the serum-factor test or pituitary testing.
  3. MRC Proof of Concept, stage one 18 Nov 2026. For turning a candidate marker into a diagnostic, or preparing a trial of a repurposed drug.
  4. MRC, under its ME/CFS highlight notice rolling. The main UK route, with decisions in June and December. MRC's first stated priority is the mechanism of post-exertional malaise.
  5. Wellcome Discovery Award rolling. Large awards for established researchers based in the UK.
  6. NIH R21 and R01 5 and 16 Feb 2027. Open to non-US institutions; the R21 (about $275k over two years) suits feasibility pilots.

Where there is no funding

  • No government call specific to ME/CFS is open in the UK, US, Canada, Norway or the Netherlands.
  • Nothing is dedicated to sampling severely ill patients at home, or to pituitary and endocrine work.
  • Little supports patient-tracking software, and almost nothing is open to developers outside universities.
  • The Open Medicine Foundation, PolyBio and Simmaron fund by invitation or partnership only.
