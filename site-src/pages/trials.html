What looks promising

No treatment has a large, replicated, placebo-controlled win in ME/CFS. These are the best bets on current evidence, grouped by how strong that evidence is. Each links to its treatment page; the numbered circles are the working model steps it acts on.

Best controlled evidence so far 9Each has at least one randomised trial with a benefit on its main outcome. None is replicated in a large blinded ME/CFS trial, and the effects are modest.
  • Pacing with heart-rate limits Reasonable to try 567Rated most helpful of over 150 treatments by ~3,925 patients (net ~75%, Eckey 2025 survey); in the first positive randomised trial, 12 weeks of coached structured pacing cut how often and how badly PEM happened vs usual care in 298 people with long COVID (PEM frequency -0.46, 95% CI -0.65 to -0.27; RECOVER-ENERGIZE, posted results, unblinded); PACE's adaptive pacing arm (n=160) was no better than usual care, but neither trial tested heart-rate-guided envelope pacing.
  • Compression garments Reasonable to try 35Randomised crossover in 16 ME/CFS patients on tilt: compression stockings cut the fall in cardiac output from 27% to 15% and in brain blood flow from 25% to 14% (van Campen 2021); this is an acute measurement, not a symptom trial.
  • Fluvoxamine Ask a doctor 67In a 399-person placebo-controlled trial in long COVID (Brazil), fluvoxamine 100 mg twice daily for 60 days reduced fatigue (FSS -0.43 at day 60, -0.58 at day 90) and improved quality of life, with fewer side effects than placebo (REVIVE, Reis 2026); a second placebo RCT (137 analysed) posted a larger fall in symptom score (-47.3 vs -31.1) without a p-value.Next readout: ESPRIT placebo RCT in long COVID (NCT07359482, n=160) recruiting
  • Antihistamines and mast-cell stabilisers (H1 + H2, cromolyn) Reasonable to try 64In STIMULATE-ICP (778 long-COVID adults, open-label), famotidine 40 mg + loratadine 10 mg cut fatigue by 1.48 points more than usual care at 12 weeks (FAS scale 10-50; colchicine gave the same), and the gain was gone 12 weeks after stopping; in ME/CFS a retrospective series found patients with mast-cell activation reported more benefit (Rohrhofer 2025).
  • Pyridostigmine Ask a doctor 35A single 60 mg dose raised peak oxygen uptake vs placebo on a repeat invasive exercise test in 45 ME/CFS patients with preload failure, largely because the placebo group got worse (Joseph 2022); a 6-10-week long-COVID RCT (n=32, conference abstract) was negative overall.Next readout: LIFT trial (pyridostigmine x low-dose naltrexone, n=160, NCT06366724), recruiting, primary completion listed as Sept 2027
  • Oxaloacetate Reasonable to try 5RESTORE ME (82 ME/CFS patients, 2 g/day for 90 days): fatigue fell 25% vs 10% on control (p=0.004), in a sponsor-involved trial (Cash 2024); a 69-person long-COVID trial missed its primary endpoint.
  • CoQ10 plus NADH Reasonable to try 5Two Spanish RCTs of CoQ10 200 mg + NADH 20 mg (n=73 and n=207) reported modest falls in fatigue scores, both from one group with industry links (Castro-Marrero 2015, 2021); CoQ10 alone at 500 mg did nothing in a 121-person long-COVID crossover trial.
  • Stimulants (methylphenidate, modafinil, lisdexamfetamine, solriamfetol) Specialist only 7Methylphenidate 10 mg twice daily beat placebo for fatigue and concentration in a 60-person 4-week crossover RCT, but only in a minority of patients (Blockmans 2006); 65% of 149 later users had stopped (Blockmans 2016); lisdexamfetamine improved executive function and fatigue vs placebo (n=26, Young 2013), and solriamfetol improved fatigue at week 8 but not week 6 (n=44, Young 2025).Next readout: Methylphenidate plus nutrient formula RCT (NCT01966276, n=134 planned) completed 2014, results never reported
  • Hyperbaric oxygen in a clinical chamber (2.0 ATA) Specialist only 456Forty sessions at 2.0 ATA beat sham for cognition and energy in a 73-person long-COVID RCT (Zilberman-Itskovich 2022), but ten sessions at 2.4 bar did no better than sham in 80 people (Kjellberg 2025); an uncontrolled Charité cohort of 30 ME/CFS patients improved after 40 sessions (SF-36 PF effect g=0.71, Kim 2026).Next readout: Sham-controlled ME/CFS RCT at Shamir (Assaf-Harofeh), Israel: 60 sessions, n=74, NCT07621068, completion ~2027
Early signals with a controlled trial now running 5Uncontrolled or retrospective results that look large. Their placebo-controlled trials are the readouts most likely to change practice.
  • Daratumumab Only in a trial 1Open-label pilot in 10 women: 6 improved markedly (SF-36 physical function 25.9 to 55.0 overall), and low NK-cell counts predicted non-response (Fluge 2025); 5 of 6 responders were reported still well at 2-3 years (conference, 2026).Next readout: ResetME randomised placebo-controlled phase II, Haukeland (n=66, CTIS 2024-520094-13-00), started 2025, ongoing
  • Low-dose aripiprazole Ask a doctor 7Retrospective review at one Stanford clinic: about 74% of 101 patients on 0.25-2 mg reported improvement in brain fog, fatigue or sleep; no control group and no randomised trial yet (Crosby 2021).Next readout: PAIS-AriSE (Charité): phase 2b placebo-controlled crossover in ~138 people with post-acute infection syndrome; protocol only (unverified)
  • Low-dose rapamycin (sirolimus) Only in a trial 16Uncontrolled decentralised study: 52 of 70 patients assessed (74%) reported improved fatigue, PEM and orthostatic intolerance on 6 mg weekly (Ruan 2025); a response rate of this size is close to the 35-40% placebo response seen in RCTs plus drop-out bias.Next readout: Mount Sinai placebo-controlled sirolimus trial in long COVID (NCT06960928, n=80), ends Nov 2026
  • IV saline infusions Specialist only 35Uncontrolled Swedish series of 22 ME/CFS patients given ~1.6 L every third week for 9 weeks: symptoms, POTS scores and quality of life improved, with effects wearing off between infusions (Sjögren 2025); no randomised trial has reported.Next readout: Saline vs placebo infusion in an ME/CFS PEM study (NCT04740736, n=106) completed July 2025, not yet reported; oral rehydration vs IV saline crossover (NCT02854683) status unknown since 2021
  • Stem cell and cord blood infusions Only in a trial 16One small industry-funded RCT: 3 infusions of donor cord blood cells (REGENECYTE) vs placebo in 30 people with long COVID (single-blind, 2:1) reported safety and a lasting fall in fatigue (Huang 2026); a 76-person RCT is overdue and five mesenchymal stem cell trials were withdrawn.Next readout: Cord blood RCT in long COVID (NCT06492798, n=76 planned) primary completion July 2025, status unknown, not reported
Tested properly and failed 10Worth knowing before anyone spends money on them. A failed trial in one population does not rule out a subgroup, but the burden of proof has moved.
  • Rituximab Avoid 1Phase III RituxME (77 vs 74, 24 months): response 26.0% on rituximab vs 35.1% on placebo (p=0.22), no secondary benefit, more serious adverse events (26% vs 19%) (Fluge 2019); no difference at 6 years (Rekeland 2024).
  • Paxlovid and SARS-CoV-2 monoclonal antibodies Avoid 1Three RCTs of 15-25 days of nirmatrelvir-ritonavir in established long COVID (~1,200 people; largest RECOVER-VITAL n=959) found no benefit on any phenotype (Baden 2026); a single dose of the antibody AER002 (n=36) also missed its primary endpoint (Peluso 2026 preprint).
  • Immunoadsorption, plasma exchange and BC007 Avoid 1Sham-controlled trials are negative: IAMPOCO (n=40, crossover; OR 1.17, p=0.77; Stortz 2026) and IA-PACS-CFS (44 vs 21, conference 2026, not yet published), after open-label series reported 50-70% responders (Stein 2025, n=20); plasma exchange was safe but no better than sham on any outcome in a 50-person RCT (España-Cueto 2025); BC007 looked positive in a 30-person crossover (Hohberger 2025), but its 119-person placebo-controlled phase 2 (NCT05911009, completed June 2024) has no posted results or paper, and the reported failure comes from the company only.
  • Efgartigimod (FcRn blocker) Only in a trial 1In 53 people with post-COVID POTS, 24 weeks of weekly infusions did no better than placebo (COMPASS-31 -14.4 vs -16.0; NCT05633407 posted results); the open-label extension was stopped early.
  • Temelimab (anti-HERV-W) Only in a trial 16In a 203-person placebo RCT in long COVID patients selected for HERV-W envelope protein in the blood, 24 weeks of monthly infusions did not reduce fatigue (PROMIS -3.2 vs -3.8) or any secondary outcome (Nehme 2026).
  • Galantamine Avoid 78A 434-person placebo RCT in ME/CFS tested three doses for 16 weeks and found no benefit on any outcome (Blacker 2004, JAMA).
  • Colchicine Only in a trial 6In a 346-person placebo RCT in long COVID (India, 26 weeks of 0.5-1 mg/day), colchicine did not improve walk distance, fatigue, quality of life or inflammation at 52 weeks (Bassi 2025); in open-label STIMULATE-ICP it cut fatigue slightly more than usual care at 12 weeks, and the gain did not last.Next readout: RECLAIM (metformin and colchicine vs usual care, NCT07280572, n=1,250 planned) enrolling
  • Metformin (for established long COVID) Only in a trial 56Two placebo RCTs in established long COVID were null: 750 mg twice daily for 60 days did not reduce fatigue (n=399 trial, Reis 2026), and 2 weeks of metformin gave the same recovery rate as placebo (64% vs 68%, n=396, Lim 2026).Next readout: RECLAIM (metformin and colchicine vs usual care, NCT07280572, n=1,250 planned) enrolling
  • Low-dose naltrexone (LDN) Ask a doctor 67The first sizeable placebo-controlled trial in post-COVID ME/CFS (160 people, up to 4.5 mg, 16 weeks; Nacul trial, NCT05430152, medRxiv preprint Oct 2026) found no difference in fatigue (FSS 4.2 vs 4.4), pain, symptom score, daily steps or quality of life; a 2026 meta-analysis found no completed RCTs, only small pre-post studies.Next readout: LIFT trial (NCT06366724, n=160, with pyridostigmine) recruiting, primary completion listed as Sept 2027; RECOVER paediatric trial ongoing
  • Graded exercise therapy (GET) Avoid 567NICE 2021 says do not offer GET or any programme of fixed incremental increases in activity; GET was the most harmful-rated treatment in the ~3,925-person survey (net about -72%), PACE's modest self-reported gains did not show on objective measures or persist, guided self-help GET gave a self-rated fatigue gain at 12 weeks (GETSET, n=211), and in children GET was no better than activity management (MAGENTA, n=241).

Readouts to watch

The running trials whose results would most change what we think or do, soonest first. Each says which step of the model it tests and what we would conclude either way. Orange means the trial has finished and no result is public yet.

A

RECLAIM: pentoxifylline and ibudilast

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 460 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT05513560

Finished, no result found
due 2025-03

460-person blinded trial (RECLAIM) of pentoxifylline, an old drug that improves red-cell flexibility and microcirculation (step 4), alongside an ibudilast arm aimed at glial inflammation (step 7). Model steps 4

If it worksFirst blinded support for a cheap, widely available microcirculation or anti-glial drug; the arm that wins says which step.

If it failsAnother blow to the 'sticky blood / microclot' branch after the rivaroxaban null.

B

IV saline vs placebo infusion

ME/CFS · placebo or sham, blinded, 106 people, objective main outcome · NCT04740736

Finished, no result found
due 2025-07

106-person blinded trial of saline vs placebo infusion in ME/CFS, measuring blood volume and exercise testing. The direct test of step 3 driving step 5. Model steps 3

Evidence so far. Uncontrolled Swedish series of 22 ME/CFS patients given ~1.6 L every third week for 9 weeks: symptoms, POTS scores and quality of life improved, with effects wearing off between infusions (Sjögren 2025); no randomised trial has reported. Treatment page

If it worksSupports low blood volume as a cause of the exercise limit; volume expansion becomes a treatment worth testing for crash prevention.

If it failsThe 'would be disproved if' test for step 3: low volume explains orthostatic symptoms but not exercise intolerance.

A

RECOVER-AUTONOMIC: ivabradine

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 181 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT06305806

Finished, awaiting result
due 2025-12

RECOVER-AUTONOMIC ivabradine arm (181). Reported at a conference: standing heart rate fell, symptoms did not. Model steps 8

Evidence so far. In RECOVER-AUTONOMIC (181 adults with long-COVID POTS, 12 weeks) ivabradine lowered the standing heart-rate rise but did not improve symptoms (ACC 2026, full paper pending); an earlier 22-person crossover in hyperadrenergic POTS was positive (Taub 2021). Treatment page

If it worksAlready reported at a meeting. The full paper may show a subgroup that benefits.

If it failsThe fast heart rate is a compensation, not the problem: consistent with step 3 coming before step 8.

C

ReMEdi: PHOE-01 (amifampridine)

ME/CFS · placebo or sham, blinded, 25 people · ISRCTN74088209

Finished, awaiting result
due 2026-03

Almost certainly amifampridine, which makes each nerve impulse release more acetylcholine, including at the autonomic ganglia that tighten veins. That is the pyridostigmine idea (steps 2-3) by a stronger route. No prior data in ME/CFS. Model steps 23

If it worksReplicate in a larger parallel trial with standing haemodynamics measured. If symptoms improve without any change in circulation, it points at nerve-to-muscle or central cholinergic signalling, which the model leaves out.

If it failsWeakens the case for boosting cholinergic transmission generally, though 2-week periods are short.

A

RECOVER-AUTONOMIC: IVIG

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 200 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT06305793

Finished, awaiting result
due 2026-06

200-person blinded RECOVER-AUTONOMIC arm testing IVIG for long COVID POTS. Tests whether autoantibodies drive the autonomic symptoms (steps 1-2). Model steps 1

Evidence so far. In autoimmune POTS, IVIG was no better than albumin (16 vs 14; COMPASS-31 -5.5 vs -10.6, p=0.63) (Vernino 2024); 1990s ME/CFS RCTs split one positive (n=49) and two negative (n=30, n=99). Treatment page

If it worksSupports an autoimmune form of post-COVID POTS; patient selection by autoantibody would be next.

If it failsTogether with the efgartigimod and immunoadsorption nulls, the autoantibody explanation for POTS weakens considerably.

A

PURE-LC: probiotic

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 194 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT06643299

Finished, awaiting result
due 2026-07

194-person blinded trial of a probiotic (PURE-LC), now completed. Gut dysbiosis and low indolepropionate replicate in four cohorts, so the gut difference is real; whether it is causal is open (step 1). Model steps 17

Evidence so far. Lactobacillus casei Shirota reduced anxiety but did not target fatigue in a 39-person RCT (Rao 2009); other studies are uncontrolled pilots. Treatment page

If it worksFirst controlled evidence that changing the microbiome changes symptoms.

If it failsGut differences may be a consequence of illness and inactivity.

Vericiguat

both · placebo or sham, blinded, 104 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT05697640

Running, recruitment closed
due 2026-12

104-person blinded trial of vericiguat, which restores nitric-oxide signalling in vessel walls, in post-COVID ME/CFS defined by Canadian criteria with PEM. A direct test of endothelial dysfunction (step 4). Model steps 45

If it worksFirst drug evidence that the vessel step matters; a mechanism-led target.

If it failsEndothelial dysfunction may be real but not limiting.

LOCITT: tirzepatide

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 1000 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT07128082

Running, recruitment closed
due 2026-12

1,000-person blinded trial of tirzepatide for long COVID fatigue, run remotely with wearables. GLP-1 drugs have anti-inflammatory and central effects (steps 6-7) and a lot of patient anecdote, but no controlled data in ME/CFS. Model steps 67

Evidence so far. No published ME/CFS study; Charité is starting an observational study of semaglutide in overweight ME/CFS patients (Solve M.E. award, Feb 2026), and RECOVER and Scripps trials are running in long COVID. Treatment page

If it worksFirst large positive drug trial in long COVID; would prompt an ME/CFS trial and a look at who responds (weight, inflammation markers).

If it failsAnecdotal benefit was weight loss or expectation; drop GLP-1 drugs from the ME/CFS shortlist.

Sirolimus (Mount Sinai)

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 80 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT06960928

Recruiting
due 2027-05

80-person placebo-controlled trial of low-dose sirolimus (Mount Sinai). The uncontrolled study reported 74% improved; this is the first control group. Model steps 16

Evidence so far. Uncontrolled decentralised study: 52 of 70 patients assessed (74%) reported improved fatigue, PEM and orthostatic intolerance on 6 mg weekly (Ruan 2025); a response rate of this size is close to the 35-40% placebo response seen in RCTs plus drop-out bias. Treatment page

Hyperbaric oxygen in ME/CFS (Shamir)

ME/CFS · placebo or sham, blinded, 74 people, objective main outcome · NCT07621068

Recruiting
due 2027-06

74-person sham-controlled trial of 60 sessions of hyperbaric oxygen in ME/CFS (Shamir, Israel), with exercise testing as the outcome. The ME/CFS test of the one positive long COVID sham trial. Model steps 56

Evidence so far. Forty sessions at 2.0 ATA beat sham for cognition and energy in a 73-person long-COVID RCT (Zilberman-Itskovich 2022), but ten sessions at 2.4 bar did no better than sham in 80 people (Kjellberg 2025); an uncontrolled Charité cohort of 30 ME/CFS patients improved after 40 sessions (SF-36 PF effect g=0.71, Kim 2026). Treatment page

If it worksHyperbaric oxygen becomes a serious option, though expensive and travel-heavy for severe patients.

If it failsWith HOT-LoCO negative, hyperbaric oxygen would be off the list.

REVERSE-LC: baricitinib

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 550 people, objective main outcome · NCT06631287

Recruiting
due 2027-07

550-person blinded trial of baricitinib, a JAK inhibitor, with an objective cognitive test as the main outcome. Tests whether ongoing interferon or cytokine signalling (steps 6-7) drives long COVID brain fog. Model steps 67

If it worksStrong evidence for an active inflammatory process in long COVID cognition; makes a JAK inhibitor trial in ME/CFS the obvious next step.

If it failsArgues against ongoing JAK-STAT cytokine signalling as the driver of cognitive symptoms.

A

LIFT: pyridostigmine and low-dose naltrexone

both · placebo or sham, blinded, 160 people, objective main outcome · NCT06366724

Recruiting
due 2027-12

The only placebo-controlled test of pyridostigmine over weeks rather than a single dose, crossed with low-dose naltrexone, and with an objective exercise outcome. Tests steps 3 and 5 directly. Model steps 3567

Evidence so far. A single 60 mg dose raised peak oxygen uptake vs placebo on a repeat invasive exercise test in 45 ME/CFS patients with preload failure, largely because the placebo group got worse (Joseph 2022); a 6-10-week long-COVID RCT (n=32, conference abstract) was negative overall. Treatment page

If it worksPyridostigmine moves from 'ask your doctor' to a standard trial for people with orthostatic intolerance; invasive CPET to find preload failure becomes worth doing.

If it failsThe single-dose result was a physiology effect that does not carry over to daily life; the preload arm of the model would need another lever (volume, compression).

RECLAIM (UK): metformin and colchicine

long COVID · randomised, not blinded, 1250 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT07280572

Running
due 2027-12

1,250-person trial of metformin plus colchicine, two drugs that have each failed alone in established long COVID. Unblinded (vs usual care) with a self-rated outcome, so a benefit would need confirming. Model steps 567

Evidence so far. Two placebo RCTs in established long COVID were null: 750 mg twice daily for 60 days did not reduce fatigue (n=399 trial, Reis 2026), and 2 weeks of metformin gave the same recovery rate as placebo (64% vs 68%, n=396, Lim 2026). Treatment page

If it worksCheck whether the effect survives in the blinded subgroups or objective outcomes before acting on it.

If it failsConfirms the single-drug failures.

ESPRIT: fluvoxamine

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 160 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT07359482

Recruiting
due 2028-05

Replication of the 399-person Brazilian trial that found less fatigue with fluvoxamine. Fluvoxamine acts on sigma-1 receptors and inflammation as well as serotonin (steps 6-7). Model steps 2

Evidence so far. In a 399-person placebo-controlled trial in long COVID (Brazil), fluvoxamine 100 mg twice daily for 60 days reduced fatigue (FSS -0.43 at day 60, -0.58 at day 90) and improved quality of life, with fewer side effects than placebo (REVIVE, Reis 2026); a second placebo RCT (137 analysed) posted a larger fall in symptom score (-47.3 vs -31.1) without a p-value. If it worksTwo positive blinded trials would make fluvoxamine the best-evidenced drug in long COVID.

If it failsThe first result was chance or site-specific; demote it.

SHIELD: valacyclovir, Paxlovid, celecoxib

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 150 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT07597902

Recruiting
due 2028-06

150-person blinded factorial trial of valacyclovir, nirmatrelvir (Paxlovid) and celecoxib. Tests reactivated herpesvirus and persistent SARS-CoV-2 (step 1) in the same people. Model steps 1

Evidence so far. In a 30-patient RCT of 6 months of valganciclovir in patients with high HHV-6 and EBV antibodies, the primary fatigue score did not differ; some secondary fatigue and cognition measures did (Montoya 2013); a 61-patient chart review found 52% responders and antibody levels did not predict response (Watt 2012). Treatment page

If it worksSupports viral persistence or reactivation; the factorial design should say which drug carries it.

If it failsAdds to the nirmatrelvir nulls and weakens the herpes reactivation idea.

Brave-LC: low-dose naltrexone in young people

long COVID · placebo or sham, blinded, 1300 people, self-rated main outcome · NCT07916259

Not yet recruiting
due 2029-01

1,300 young people, phase 3, blinded. Low-dose naltrexone failed in the 160-person adult trial, so this is now a test of whether younger people respond differently. Model steps 67

Evidence so far. The first sizeable placebo-controlled trial in post-COVID ME/CFS (160 people, up to 4.5 mg, 16 weeks; Nacul trial, NCT05430152, medRxiv preprint Oct 2026) found no difference in fatigue (FSS 4.2 vs 4.4), pain, symptom score, daily steps or quality of life; a 2026 meta-analysis found no completed RCTs, only small pre-post studies. ResetME: daratumumab

ME/CFS · placebo or sham, blinded, 66 people, self-rated main outcome · 2024-520094-13-00

Running

The randomised placebo-controlled follow-up (66 people, Haukeland) to the open-label pilot where 6 of 10 women improved markedly. Tests whether long-lived plasma cells and their antibodies keep the illness going (step 1). Model steps 1

Evidence so far. Open-label pilot in 10 women: 6 improved markedly (SF-36 physical function 25.9 to 55.0 overall), and low NK-cell counts predicted non-response (Fluge 2025); 5 of 6 responders were reported still well at 2-3 years (conference, 2026). Treatment page

If it worksThe strongest evidence yet for an antibody-driven subgroup; NK-cell count could be used to select patients.

If it failsAfter rituximab, a second failure of B-lineage depletion would make an antibody cause much less likely.

Design and results

The better a trial is designed, the less often it finds a benefit. Most uncontrolled trials report one; most large blinded trials do not. Of 257 trials that finished more than a year ago, we found no published result.
