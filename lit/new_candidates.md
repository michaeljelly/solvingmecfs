# Candidate new findings (relation=new, importance >= 2)

## immune (1)
- i2 Petty 2016 [ME/CFS] (?, case-control): PBMC microarray found 34 miRNAs up-regulated in CFS/ME, 4 (miR-99b, miR-330, miR-126, miR-30c) confirmed by qRT-PCR and replicated in an independent cohort, most strongly in NK cells; transfecting miR-99b or miR-330-3p into NK cells produced activation with reduced cytotoxicity gene signatures. https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0150904

## infection (1)
- i3 Reis 2026 [long COVID] (399, RCT): In an adaptive placebo-controlled RCT of 399 long-COVID adults with fatigue in Brazil, fluvoxamine 100 mg BID lowered FSS vs placebo by 0.43 at day 60 and 0.58 at day 90 (posterior probability of superiority 99%) and improved quality of life; metformin was inconclusive and stopped for futility. https://doi.org/10.7326/annals-26-02144

## muscle-exertion (2)
- i2 Matits 2026 [long COVID] (228 (128 vs 100), case-control): In 128 PASC vs 100 recovered controls, PASC had lower relative circulating cell-free mtDNA, higher CRP (25% low-grade inflammation) and worse cognition; ccf-mtDNA correlated positively with general cognition and negatively with CRP, but CRP differences did not survive confounder adjustment. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-026-03471-0
- i2 Carpallo-Porcar 2026, preprint [long COVID] (?, RCT): In a double-blind RCT of 144 PCC patients, an SPM-enriched marine oil supplement plus 12-week telerehabilitation was no better than placebo plus telerehabilitation on EQ-5D-5L or any secondary outcome (p > .05); both arms improved within-group (e.g. +39.7 m 6MWT in the supplement arm). https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-9354365/v1

## neuro (1)
- i2 Wirth 2026 [ME/CFS] (6077 vs 5x, case-control): In German insurance data on 6,077 people aged 6-27 with ME/CFS matched 1:5 to controls, 44 ICD-10 code classes from the previous year raised the odds of a later ME/CFS diagnosis, including fatigue, depression, pain and somatoform disorders (OR 1.11-2.19), fibromyalgia (OR 2.08), MCI (OR 2.93) and post-COVID condition (OR 3.84, 2.97-4.98). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-40848-1

## omics (1)
- i2 Maloney 2010 [ME/CFS] (111 CFS, 259 ISF, 123 controls, case-control): In a Georgia population-based study (111 CFS, 259 ISF, 123 controls), CFS had twice the odds of ATP III metabolic syndrome (OR 2.12, 95% CI 1.06-4.23), each extra component raised CFS odds by 37%, and component count correlated with fatigue (r=0.20, p=0.04). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.metabol.2009.12.019

## vascular (4)
- i2 Wu 2026 [long COVID] (108, case-control): In 108 patients with post-COVID headache and/or brain fog vs symptom-free post-COVID controls, anterior Focal Cerebral Arteriopathy Severity Score rose with symptom categories (combined 6.6 vs headache 3.6 vs brain fog 4.7 vs control 0.8), suggesting anterior circulation arteriopathy and hypoperfusion in brain fog. https://doi.org/10.1007/s00406-026-02276-0
- i2 Sommen 2026 [long COVID] (?, case-control): In 12-25-year-old females followed 6 months after mild SARS-CoV-2, CyTOF found more hyperresponsive terminal NK cells, more activated/exhausted PD-1+ CD4 effector T cells and fewer marginal-zone and transitional B cells in both long COVID and fatigued uninfected controls, with no LC-specific immune change; terminal NK cells were the top machine-learning predictor of fatigue. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08149-3
- i2 Wang 2025 [long COVID] (about 70, RCT): In a sham-controlled randomised trial of 57 post-COVID POTS patients, 1 month of low-level tragus stimulation reduced the supine-to-stand HR rise and average/max HR, lowered plasma NPY, and increased HF and decreased LF and LF/HF HRV. https://doi.org/10.1002/clc.70110
- i2 Abd-Eldayem 2026, preprint [long COVID] (?, case-control): Long COVID POTS patients, versus recovered non-POTS controls, had higher monocyte mitochondrial content and superoxide, lower NRF2 antioxidant enzymes, more monocyte isolevuglandins, 3-fold more circulating T cell-monocyte doublets with IFN-g/IL-17A-producing effector T cells correlating with severity, and raised IL-17A, IFN-g and TNF-a; tVNS reduced doublets, IsoLGs and monocyte IL-6 in a subset. https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.05.08.26352776


# Verified results with no matching atlas finding (importance >= 2)

- i2 St-Jean 2026, preprint [ME/CFS]: k-modes clustering of 19,019 DecodeME participants gave a high symptom burden cluster (57%) and lower burden cluster (43%); infectious and unknown onset had aOR 1.24 and 1.30 for high-burden membership vs non-infectious onset; no genetic variant was associated with cluster membership. https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.06.29.26356818
- i2 Af Geijerstam 2026 [long COVID]: Among 208,050 Swedish women, more than 8 pre-pandemic symptom-based primary-care visits predicted later PCC (OR 5.45), PVFS (OR 7.71) and exhaustion disorder (OR 5.32) dose-dependently, though 17% of PCC cases had no such visits. https://doi.org/10.1080/02813432.2025.2611886
- i2 Fall 2024 [ME/CFS]: In MCAM (595 ME/CFS vs 328 controls), 76% vs 17.4% had at least one chronic overlapping pain condition: migraine/headache 48.1%, fibromyalgia 45.0% (PR 147.7), low back pain 33.1% (PR 39.5), IBS 31.6%. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12883-024-03872-0
- i2 Boruch 2024 [ME/CFS]: In 40 Gulf War Illness veterans, a randomised crossover of seated rest vs light, moderate and vigorous 20-min cycling showed no condition-by-time interaction for symptoms, pain sensitivity, cognition or most inflammatory markers within 1 h, and no effect on actigraphy over 7 days. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbi.2024.05.026
- i2 Antar 2023 [long COVID]: In 73 non-hospitalised adults sampled serially, brain fog (aRR 0.46, 95% CI 0.22-0.95) and muscle pain (aRR 0.28, 0.08-0.94) at 90+ days were associated with failure to clear upper-airway SARS-CoV-2 RNA within 28 days of acute onset. https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2023.1147549
- i2 Royston 2023 [ME/CFS]: UK/Ireland paediatric surveillance found 33 severe, 4 probable and 55 possible severe ME/CFS cases aged 5-16, a prevalence of 3.2 per million (8.9 including possible/probable) and incidence of 0.90 per million child-years, median age 13, 58% female. https://doi.org/10.1136/archdischild-2022-324319
- i2 Baum 2022 [long COVID]: In 227 post-COVID syndrome patients, most had normal echocardiographic cardiac function; fatigue (MFI-20) did not correlate with echo findings or biomarkers, and only the PCFS 4 subgroup and patients with raised troponin T showed impaired GLS, lower LVEF and TAPSE. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-24038-3
- i2 Nkiliza 2021 [ME/CFS]: In 50 ME/CFS vs 50 controls (half male), females had lower total PE, arachidonic-PE and hexosylceramides, males had higher HexCer, MUFA-PE, PI, saturated TG and linoleic-derived oxylipins, and PC-dominated components correlated negatively with headache and fatigue severity. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-021-03035-6
- i2 Barhorst 2020 [ME/CFS]: Meta-analysis of 37 studies (1016 patients, 686 controls): RPE during acute aerobic exercise was higher in ME/CFS and FM than controls (Hedges d 0.85, 95% CI 0.62-1.08). https://doi.org/10.1249/mss.0000000000002421
- i2 Schoeman 2017 [ME/CFS]: Complete mtDNA sequencing of 93 UK and South African CFS patients found none of the more than 200 clinically proven pathogenic mtDNA mutations. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12881-017-0387-6
- i2 Collin 2016 [ME/CFS]: Latent class analysis in 7,041 UK and 1,392 Dutch CFS clinic patients gave up to 6 symptom phenotypes (full polysymptomatic 32.8%, pain-only 20.3%, oligosymptomatic 4.7%); a 3-class solution replicated in the Dutch cohort, and polysymptomatic patients were more severe and more comorbid. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpsychores.2015.12.006
- i2 Maes 2015 [ME/CFS]: In 139 ME/CFS, 65 chronic fatigue and 40 controls, CD8+CD38+ and CD8+HLA-DR+ proportions were higher in ME/CFS, CD19+ and the CD4/CD8 ratio were lower, and none of it related to severity, duration, O&NS, leaky-gut or cytokine markers. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26707044/
- i2 Newton 2011 [ME/CFS]: 107 Fukuda CFS patients had sedentary time similar to 107 matched sedentary controls but less time above 3 METs (79% under 10,000 steps/day), and the lower activity was partly explained by HRV-indexed autonomic dysfunction, not by fatigue severity. https://doi.org/10.1093/qjmed/hcr029
- i2 Evering 2011 [ME/CFS]: Systematic review of 17 studies: 14 found lower physical activity in CFS; meta-analysis of 7 studies put daily activity at 68% of controls, with no significant difference in variability (CV 34.3% vs 31.5%). https://doi.org/10.1177/0269215510380831
- i2 Altemus 2001 [ME/CFS]: In 19 CFS patients vs 19 matched controls, a 1-h arginine vasopressin infusion produced a reduced ACTH response and a more rapid cortisol response in CFS, interpreted as reduced hypothalamic CRH secretion. https://doi.org/10.1016/s0306-4530(00)00044-5
- i2 Scott 1999 [ME/CFS]: The delta-ACTH response to 100 ug ovine CRH was attenuated in CFS (21.0 vs 57.8 ng/L, p significant, df=21), and adding 10 ug desmopressin augmented and normalised the pituitary-adrenal response in CFS. https://doi.org/10.1016/s0006-3223(98)00232-7
- i2 Swanink 1996 [ME/CFS]: In 76 CFS patients vs 69 controls, CD11b on CD8 cells was decreased, previously reported CD38 and HLA-DR increases were not confirmed, and no marker correlated with fatigue severity. https://doi.org/10.1093/infdis/173.2.460
- i2 Swanink 1994 [ME/CFS]: In 76 patients with disabling unexplained fatigue and 76 matched controls, enterovirus serology and VP1 antigen testing did not differ, no enterovirus grew from stool culture, and stool PCR was positive in one patient only, and not on a repeat sample 3 months later. https://doi.org/10.1093/clinids/19.5.860
