ME/CFS in one page

What defines the illness, how it is diagnosed, how severe it gets, how it starts and how often people recover. Written for researchers coming to the field.

Updated 8 Nov 2026 · Numbers link to the evidence atlas, where each is graded with its sources.

Myalgic encephalomyelitis/chronic fatigue syndrome (ME/CFS) is a chronic illness defined by post-exertional malaise (PEM): physical, cognitive or emotional effort that a healthy person would shrug off triggers a worsening of all symptoms, usually starting hours to a day later and lasting days to weeks. Around this sit unrefreshing sleep, cognitive impairment, intolerance of being upright, pain and a large loss of function. Most cases start after an infection. Long COVID with PEM is, as far as anyone can tell, the same illness, and has added millions of new cases since 2020.

Post-exertional malaise

PEM is what separates ME/CFS from other fatiguing illnesses, and the reason exercise-based rehabilitation harms many patients. After a two-day exercise test, patients took a mean of about 13 days to recover, against about 2 days for sedentary controls (source). Onset, length and worst symptom differ from person to person. For research this has two consequences: a sample taken at rest misses the state that defines the illness, and any study visit is itself an exertion. The working model is built around explaining PEM, and sampling during a crash is the experiment we rank first.

How it is diagnosed

There is no diagnostic test. Diagnosis rests on the symptom pattern after ruling out other causes, such as thyroid, adrenal and autoimmune disease, sleep disorders, anaemia and depression. Criteria differ mainly on whether PEM is required, and the criteria a study uses decide who is in it: studies using Fukuda include many people without PEM.

Severity

MildRoughly half of normal activity. Often still working or studying, with no energy left for anything else.
ModerateReduced mobility, usually stopped working. Needs long rests and pays for any extra effort.
SevereMostly housebound or bedbound. Short trips out cause long crashes. Often sensitive to light and sound.
Very severeBedbound and dependent on care. Some cannot tolerate light, sound, touch or conversation, and need help to eat.

After the NICE 2021 definitions. Severe patients are the least studied: the atlas shows how few have been sampled, and the project register shows how few current projects name them.

How it starts and how it goes

Most patients date their illness to an infection. In a prospective Australian cohort, about 11% of people with laboratory-confirmed EBV, Q fever or Ross River virus met criteria six months later, at the same rate for all three pathogens, and the severity of the acute illness predicted who did (source). That points at the host response rather than the particular microbe. Risk runs in families: first-degree relatives have two to three times the risk (source), and the DecodeME genome-wide study found eight risk loci, concentrated in neuronal genes (genetics).

Once established, full recovery in adults is uncommon; improvement is more common, and adolescents do better (source). All-cause mortality was not raised in a London cohort, but suicide mortality was about seven times expected (source). No treatment has a large, positive, placebo-controlled trial (trials).

History of the field

  1. 1955Outbreak among staff at the Royal Free Hospital, London. "Benign myalgic encephalomyelitis" enters the literature over the following years, after similar clusters elsewhere.
  2. 1969WHO classifies benign myalgic encephalomyelitis as a neurological disease (now ICD code G93.3).
  3. 1984–85Cluster around Lake Tahoe, Nevada. The CDC names "chronic fatigue syndrome" in its 1988 case definition.
  4. 1994Fukuda criteria, the research standard for the next two decades. PEM is optional.
  5. 2003Canadian Consensus Criteria make PEM mandatory.
  6. 2009–11The XMRV retrovirus claim is shown to be lab contamination and retracted. PACE reports modest benefit from CBT and graded exercise; re-analyses later find the effects confined to self-report.
  7. 2015The US Institute of Medicine report calls ME/CFS a serious systemic disease and sets new criteria built around PEM. Rituximab looks promising in Norwegian phase II trials.
  8. 2017NIH funds the Collaborative Research Centers. Rituximab fails its phase III trial in 2019.
  9. 2020COVID-19. A large share of long COVID patients develop PEM and meet ME/CFS criteria.
  10. 2021NICE NG206 removes graded exercise therapy from UK guidance.
  11. 2024The NIH intramural deep-phenotyping study is published, and its "effort preference" interpretation is widely disputed.
  12. 2025DecodeME reports the first replicated genetic risk loci, from 15,579 cases. RECOVER publishes ME/CFS incidence after COVID.
